# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Waypoint is committed to a respectful, inclusive, and technically constructive
community. Everyone participating in the project is expected to help create an
environment where people can contribute without harassment, intimidation, or abuse.

This standard applies regardless of experience, identity, background, employer,
citizenship, security clearance, or relationship to the project.

## Expected behavior

- Treat other participants with patience, respect, and good faith.
- Critique ideas and changes without attacking the people proposing them.
- Give actionable feedback and accept correction gracefully.
- Respect privacy, operational-security boundaries, and requests to stop an unwanted
  interaction.
- Be honest about test results, limitations, conflicts of interest, and mistakes.
- Help keep discussions relevant to the project and welcoming to new contributors.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, or demeaning comments.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention.
- Trolling, sustained disruption, deliberate misrepresentation, or personal attacks.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Pressuring anyone to disclose employer, customer, government, laboratory, identity,
  credential, or security-sensitive information.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or participates in its review.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  community.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other
project spaces. It also applies when someone is representing Waypoint publicly, such
as by using an official project account or speaking on behalf of the project.

## Reporting

Do not report sensitive conduct concerns in a public issue. Contact the repository
maintainer privately through the contact methods on their GitHub profile. If private
maintainer contact is unavailable or inappropriate, use GitHub's reporting tools.

Include only the information needed to understand the concern. Do not include
credentials, private infrastructure details, controlled information, or unrelated
personal data. Reports will be handled as discreetly as practical, although complete
confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where safety or law requires disclosure.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this code. They
may edit or remove contributions and may warn, temporarily restrict, or permanently
ban participants whose behavior is incompatible with these expectations.

Enforcement decisions will consider severity, context, impact, prior behavior, and
whether the participant takes responsibility and corrects the problem. Maintainers
with a conflict of interest should recuse themselves when another qualified reviewer
is available.

Good-faith reports will not result in retaliation. Knowingly false or malicious
reports may themselves violate this code.
